# Story Bridge — English narration script

**Target runtime:** 5:00  
**Footage:** screenshots of the live public site and its public stories. No generated images are used.  
**Narration:** English voice synthesized locally with the installed Microsoft Mark voice; no external audio service is used.

## Opening — 0:00

Welcome to Story Bridge. This is a place to share cultural stories in the language they were told, and to listen across communities. Visitors can explore public stories. Stories, cultures, search and museum collections are brought together, while community voices remain distinct from museum records.

## Discover — 0:20

Discover brings public stories together. Each story card connects the writer's words to the language, place and photographs they chose to share. As we move down the feed, we meet eight different contributors and eight different perspectives. Erwin Mashobane writes about the Mashobane clan and its heritage. Nosihle Mavundla tells us about the pride and traditions of the Zulu nation. Scarlet shares *Kobo ya Sesotho*, a story about the Basotho blanket. Jenovic Kb's French-language story is called *Dompteur de Taureau*. Nathan Kabange writes about the Sanga and Katanga. Samantha Mulungisi celebrates being Zimbabwean. Lwamba Moket Dan shares the story of a monument of Katanga identity in Lubumbashi. Josue Kabuya documents the painted walls of the Southern Ndebele. Visitors can use the language, audio and region filters, then open a post to read the full account. Place and language labels make it easier to explore without changing a storyteller's own words. The collection is still growing, one contribution at a time. These stories come from different contributors and reflect customs, belonging, family histories and cultural memory. The cards offer a starting point for listening. Public posts can be read by visitors without signing in.

## Open a story — 1:45

Opening a story gives the full account more room. Here, Josue's Ndebele research note keeps its original language, region, author and photo credit together. Sources and the story record help readers see where the account comes from. The translation status is also clear. Text remains easy to follow.

## Read across languages — 2:06

Readers can choose between an author-written translation and machine translation. The French version here is the author's work, marked as not yet checked by a speaker. For other languages, Read in my language uses Chrome's on-device translator when available, or opens Google Translate. The control is labelled machine translation, and the original text remains available. Readers can return to the original and compare versions whenever they need to. The translation remains clearly identified.

## Cultures and search — 2:39

Cultures organizes the directory by region and country. Each entry leads to a page with an introduction, photographs and credit. The page can show languages used in that country and connect to public stories. It also tells readers when community knowledge has not yet been added, instead of inventing detail. Search brings stories and cultures together. In this example, Ndebele returns the culture page and two related stories. A visitor can follow the same subject across different sources and voices. Search suggestions offer other routes into the catalogue, and the filters help narrow results. This makes the collection easier to explore while leaving each story in its own words. The Ndebele detail page links languages and public stories to this culture entry.

## Collections — 3:29

Collections is the museum catalogue, kept separate from community stories. Each object record shows where the object is held and what its sources say. The notes are careful about limits: an object cannot tell us every part of a community's living practice or history. The catalogue offers useful context, but it does not stand in for people sharing their own knowledge. Some records also include translations so readers can learn across languages. A translation toggle makes those versions easy to compare.

## Sharing and care — 4:04

To share a story, a visitor first signs in. The guided flow begins with language, then writing, translation and review. The storyteller can choose Public, Members only or Only me, and can check details before submitting. A first post may wait for a moderator. Community Guidelines set expectations for consent, accurate context, photo credit and care around sacred or restricted knowledge.

## Mobile — 4:33

The phone view keeps Discover and the main navigation within reach, with the same public stories ready to browse. The layout remains familiar on a smaller screen.

## Closing — 4:45

Story Bridge grows through the voices people choose to share. Read carefully, follow the sources, and keep listening. Every voice adds to a shared record. Share what you know, and learn from every other.

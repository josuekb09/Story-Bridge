# Object documentation — Luluwa (Lulua) objects

Project research notes for Story Bridge. Compiled by Josue Kabuya from museum records and published scholarship. **This is not a community account.** Community stories are shown separately, with their authors.

Rule used throughout: every statement below is tied to a source listed under it. Where a source is silent, we write "Not recorded" and do not fill the gap. Where we offer a reading of our own, it is labelled **Our reading**.

## 1. The Luluwa and their art (context for all four objects)

**English**
The Luluwa (also written Lulua, Bena Lulua) live between the Lubudi and Kasai rivers in the south-central Democratic Republic of the Congo. They are known for carved male and female figures, masks and decorative arts. The art historian Constantine Petridis, whose 2018 book is the main modern study, places the creation of the best-known Luluwa art in the second half of the 19th century.
Luluwa sculpture is most often a human figure with a fully scarified body. The Canadian Museum of History notes that this statuary flourished for a short period at the end of the 19th and the start of the 20th century, even though the Luluwa themselves had by then stopped practising that scarification. Masks are rarer than figures.
In the 1880s a new movement spread under the chief Kalamba-Mukenge, centred on smoking cannabis (*dyamba*), meant to promote peace, joy and eternal life (Petridis, citing Wissmann's account).

**What we cannot know from museum objects.** Petridis writes that when he travelled in Luluwa country in the mid-1990s, the traditional "historical" art had almost entirely disappeared. He notes that this makes it hard to reconstruct the functions and meanings of works kept in Western collections, removed from their homeland more than fifty years earlier. For that reason Story Bridge does not state what any object "means" or "was used for" unless a source says so.

**Français**
Les Luluwa (aussi Lulua, Bena Lulua) vivent entre les rivières Lubudi et Kasaï, dans le centre-sud de la République démocratique du Congo. Ils sont connus pour leurs statues masculines et féminines, leurs masques et leurs arts décoratifs. Selon Constantine Petridis, dont le livre de 2018 est la principale étude récente, les œuvres les plus connues datent vraisemblablement de la seconde moitié du XIXᵉ siècle.
La statuaire luluwa représente le plus souvent un corps humain entièrement scarifié. Le Musée canadien de l'histoire rappelle qu'elle a fleuri pendant une courte période, à la fin du XIXᵉ et au début du XXᵉ siècle, alors que les Luluwa avaient déjà cessé cette scarification. Les masques sont plus rares que les statues.
Dans les années 1880, un mouvement religieux autour de la fumée de chanvre (*dyamba*), censé favoriser la paix, la joie et la vie éternelle, s'est répandu sous le chef Kalamba-Mukenge (Petridis, d'après le récit de Wissmann).

**Ce que les objets de musée ne disent pas.** Petridis écrit que, lors de ses voyages en pays luluwa au milieu des années 1990, l'art traditionnel « historique » avait presque entièrement disparu. Il est donc difficile de reconstituer la fonction et le sens des œuvres conservées dans les collections occidentales. Story Bridge n'affirme donc pas ce qu'un objet « signifie » ou à quoi il « servait », sauf si une source le dit.

## 2. Statue — Ethnologisches Museum Berlin (inv. III C 3246)

**What the museum record says**
- Male figure, wood and pigment, height 74 cm, 19th century. Kept at the Ethnologisches Museum, Berlin. Inventory number III C 3246.
- Commons description (French): effigy of a chief wearing a leopard-skin loincloth, *mukalenga wa nkashaama*; "acquired in the field, in the village of Tshiweewe, in 1885".
- Commons description (German/English): from the collection of Hermann von Wissmann, "acquired 1886".

**The two dates.** The two descriptions give 1885 and 1886. **Our reading** (not stated by any source): Wissmann crossed the Kasai region between 1883 and 1885, so 1885 may be the field acquisition and 1886 the museum's entry. Berlin records for other Wissmann objects also show 1886. Treat this as a hypothesis until the museum confirms.

**Who was Wissmann.** Hermann von Wissmann (1853–1905) led expeditions in the Kasai region in 1883–85 and published *Im Innern Afrikas* (1888; 3rd ed. 1891). The Berlin museum lists him as collector of many Kasai objects.

**What we do not know.** Who made it, who the chief represented, what the village of Tshiweewe's people said about it, and the terms of the acquisition. "Not recorded."

**Sources**
- Commons file page (author Ji-Elle, CC BY-SA 3.0): https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Statue_Luluwa-Mus%C3%A9e_ethnologique_de_Berlin.jpg
- Staatliche Museen zu Berlin, Wissmann as collector: https://search.smb.museum/en/object/obj-1495190
- Wissmann et al., *Im Innern Afrikas* (cited in Tribal Art Magazine): https://mag.tribalartmagazine.com/T89ENtop/page_103.html

**Français (note courte)**
Figure masculine en bois et pigment, 74 cm, XIXᵉ siècle, Ethnologisches Museum de Berlin, inv. III C 3246. La description française de Commons parle d'une effigie de chef au pagne en peau de léopard (*mukalenga wa nkashaama*), acquise sur place à Tshiweewe en 1885 ; la version allemande/anglaise indique la collection Hermann von Wissmann, acquise en 1886. **Notre lecture** : 1885 serait l'acquisition sur le terrain et 1886 l'entrée au musée, mais aucune source ne le confirme. L'auteur, le sens et les conditions de l'acquisition : non documentés.

## 4. Figure of a Squatting Male — Brooklyn Museum (22.1448)

**What the museum record says** (Brooklyn Museum online record)
- Title: Figure of a Squatting Male. Attribution: "Lulua; or Nsapo-Nsapo" — the museum itself lists two possible attributions.
- Date: early 20th century. Place made: Democratic Republic of the Congo. Medium: wood.
- **Size: 4½ × 1¼ × 1¼ in (11.4 × 3.2 × 3.2 cm).** This is a very small figure, not a life-size statue.
- Credit line: Museum Expedition 1922, Robert B. Woodward Memorial Fund. Not on view.
- Provenance: **not published** on the page.

**Correction to our earlier wording.** We had written "collected in the Museum Expedition of 1922". The record only gives a *credit line*. For a related Brooklyn object with the same credit line (22.107), the museum documents that it was bought in Brussels from François Poncelet by Stewart Culin in 1922. So the credit line does **not** by itself mean the object was collected in the field in Congo. For 22.1448 the route is not published.

**Invitation.** The museum's page asks anyone with information about an object to contact bkmcollections@brooklynmuseum.org. Community members who recognise a figure like this are invited to tell their own story on Story Bridge and, if they wish, to write to the museum.

**Sources**
- Brooklyn Museum record: https://www.brooklynmuseum.org/objects/22327
- Related record 22.107 with provenance: https://www.brooklynmuseum.org/objects/141965
- Commons file page (Brooklyn Museum, CC BY 3.0): https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Brooklyn_Museum_22.1448_Figure_of_a_Squatting_Male.jpg

## 6. Open questions for the community

These are the gaps. A story from someone who knows is worth more than any of our notes:
1. What do Luluwa and neighbouring communities say about chief effigies such as the Berlin figure?
2. Do people recognise the Tshiweewe village and its history?
3. Who made, owned or honoured objects like the Île d'Aix statuette?
4. Is the Brooklyn figure's attribution (Lulua or Nsapo-Nsapo) known locally?
5. Which Luluwa masks were made, by whom, and for what occasions?

Anyone can answer in their own language, in their own voice, under "Speak about this object".
